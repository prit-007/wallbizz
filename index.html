Inside the app

Four stops, from first open to "set as wallpaper"

Pulled straight from how the app is actually built — no invented feature list.

TrendingAnimeNatureCyberpunk
01 — Discover

Seven category pills, one grid that never restarts

Trending, Anime, Nature, Cyberpunk, Space, Desktop and Mobile sit above a staggered masonry grid. Each tab loads on first visit and stays alive in the background — switch categories and come back, and your scroll position is exactly where you left it.

  • Shimmer skeletons while a category loads
  • Tap the active tab again to jump back to the top

02 — Detail

The wallpaper sets the mood of its own screen

Pinch to zoom, double-tap for a snappy 2.5x, and drag down past a quarter of the screen to close — or it springs back if you let go early. A blurred, tinted copy of the image fills the background, so the frame around it always matches what's inside it.

  • Tap once to hide the bars for a clean, full-screen look
  • Specs card shows resolution, size, and the image's own palette
Search by resolution or ID
03 — Save it

A wishlist, moodboards, and a Vault of your own

Tap the heart to add something to your wishlist, or drop it into a named moodboard — both sync per account through Supabase. Every download also lands in the Vault, tracked locally, searchable by ID or resolution, with long-press multi-select for cleaning house.

  • Sign-in is only asked for when you actually save something
  • Swipe to delete, or select several at once

04 — Make it yours

Crop, rotate, and set it without leaving the app

The wallpaper editor lets you pan, zoom, and rotate in 90° steps before applying it directly to your device. Sharing overlays a small WALLBIZZ watermark and cleans up the temp file after. Settings covers dark mode, where downloads land (Pictures, Downloads, or app-only storage), and account.

  • Choose whether downloads survive an uninstall
  • Sign in with email or Google, only when you need to
Under the hood

The app never talks to Wallhaven directly for browsing

A Go service syncs a fresh batch into Supabase twice a day. The Flutter client reads from Supabase; Wallhaven is only touched for live search, and NSFW queries route through the backend with your session attached.
